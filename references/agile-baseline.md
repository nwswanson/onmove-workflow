# Agile baseline for squad assessment

Use these principles as defaults for interpretation and coaching. Apply explicit organizational requirements and authorized squad agreements before prescribing local mechanics. Do not assume that using Jira means adopting Scrum or Kanban in full.

## Outcomes before ceremony labels

Ask whether work produces a useful result, whether the next decisions are sufficiently understood, whether quality is established, and whether the team learns from evidence. A meeting title, ticket count, or busy activity stream is insufficient proof.

The Agile Manifesto principles emphasize useful software, collaboration, sustainable pace, and regular adjustment. Translate these into questions about delivery, feedback, support, and improvement rather than an automatic ceremony checklist.

Primary source: [Principles behind the Agile Manifesto](https://agilemanifesto.org/principles.html).

## Apply Scrum distinctions accurately

- Treat refinement as ongoing clarification and decomposition of backlog work. Do not require a particular meeting merely because the baseline recommends refinement.
- Distinguish refinement's clarification purpose from planning the next Sprint's goal and work. Recognize that refinement may also occur during Sprint Planning.
- Evaluate a reported implementation against the applicable Definition of Done before describing it as verified completion.
- Distinguish inspection of product outcomes from reflection on how the team works.
- Preserve team autonomy within explicit organizational standards and agreements.

These distinctions do not authorize changing the user's required practices. If the manager explicitly required a readiness discussion, determine whether its intended output occurred even when the squad used a different agenda or name.

Primary source: [The 2020 Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html), especially Sprint Planning, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective, Product Backlog, and Definition of Done.

## Interpret flow in the squad's actual workflow

Establish what a work item is and where work starts and finishes before calculating or interpreting flow. Preserve local status mappings, work-item types, and explicit policies.

The Kanban Guide identifies work in progress, throughput, work-item age, and cycle time as flow measures. Interpret them against the team's workflow and use them to inform decisions. A last-edit timestamp is not a work-start timestamp.

Use blocked or aging work to ask what intervention would help. Do not equate item counts with delivered value or infer poor individual performance from slow-moving work. Avoid comparisons whose definitions or populations differ.

Primary source: [The Kanban Guide, May 2025](https://kanbanguides.org/the-kanban-guide/2025.5/), especially Definition of Workflow and Flow Metrics.

## Distinguish requirements, adaptations, and observations

| Record | Example | Handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Required outcome | Resolve acceptance questions before committing scope | Keep authority and explicit exceptions. |
| Default recommendation | Inspect aging work regularly | Recommend or ask; do not invent a mandate. |
| Authorized local adaptation | Squad refines asynchronously before planning | Check the intended output and applicable boundaries. |
| Observed deviation | Sprint selection occurred while key questions remained unresolved | Record evidence, impact, uncertainty, and follow-up. |
| Recurring pattern | The same output gap appears across dated instances | Retain instances and counterexamples; propose a coaching experiment. |

Do not turn observations into agreements without an authorized decision. Do not assume malice or incompetence when a mistake, interruption, unclear instruction, or conflicting goal could explain the evidence.

## Coach with an observable feedback loop

Describe the behavior and its consequence, identify the unresolved outcome, and propose a concrete intervention. Record the owner, acceptance state, and next review trigger. On review, look for evidence that the intended change occurred; do not merely repeat the original advice.

Keep the manager's own clarifications and check-ins alongside squad obligations. Preserve open obligations until they are evidenced as complete, explicitly cancelled, or superseded.

These assessment and coaching procedures are design choices for this skill; they are not claims that an Agile guide mandates the specific record structure or intervention.
